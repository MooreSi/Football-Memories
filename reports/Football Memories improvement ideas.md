# Turn existing tags into safer, richer memories

Football Memories already holds the inputs that commercial products spend AI money to create. The biggest next gains come from cheap features on data the app already stores, not from computer vision. Game-state timelines, comebacks, first-goal records and "on this day" anniversaries are exact facts that need only goal events with timestamps and dates. A fact-gated match recap and a season "Wrapped" story reuse the same facts. Smart playlists turn tags into reels that keep growing. Alongside these sit two protective features that matter more than any analytics: a source-expiry queue for Veo recordings, which Veo deletes after 6 or 12 months, and scheduled multi-copy fixity checks. Both matter because the local archive is often the only copy. Vendors' per-player features (Veo Player Spotlight, Trace PlayerFocus, Hudl Automatic Athlete Playlists) all depend on shirt numbers or tagged jersey data. The app can match them with a per-match "his number" field plus tag overlap, and needs no faces or biometrics. Vision work (action spotting, tracking, vertical reframing, semantic search) is real but brittle on amateur footage. It must run as a native macOS worker, so it belongs in a second phase that suggests rather than decides.

## Commercial products converge on per-player reels, AI recaps and shareable stories

The 2025-2026 product launches agree on a feature set. Veo's Player Spotlight reads shirt numbers to build each player's playlist and lets users tune pre/post-roll and distance-to-ball sensitivity ([Veo](https://shop.veo.co/pages/what-is-veo-player-spotlight)). "Circle the Player" (spring 2025) rings a chosen player during key actions ([Veo](https://www.veo.co/article/circle-the-player-q-a-with-linda-skovmand-and-signe-bering)). Trace separates **Spotlight highlights** (the child is the focus) from **Tactical highlights** (moments that matter to the child even off-frame, such as a team goal). It also offers a following circle, a following name label and a zoom mode ([Trace support](https://support.traceup.com/content/playerfocus-highlights-and-always-on-spotlight)). Hudl's August 2025 soccer update added **AI-written match summaries linked to video** and **Automatic Athlete Playlists** ([Hudl](https://www.hudl.com/blog/whats-new-soccer)). Hudl also exports highlights in **9:16 vertical** ([Hudl Balltime](https://www.hudl.com/support/balltime/guides/highlights)). Spiideo's May 2026 AI Highlights turn one game into a **Game Recap, Home Team Story, Away Team Story, MVP Story and Halftime Highlights** ([Sports Video Group](https://www.sportsvideo.org/2026/05/07/spiideo-launches-ai-highlights-for-automated-highlight-and-clip-creation/)). GameChanger writes newspaper-style recaps from scoring data ([GameChanger help](https://help.gc.com/hc/en-us/articles/360045621491)).

Each of these maps onto data Football Memories already has. The app has a `shirt_number` field and Hudl player-name codes, so it can split the child's moments the way Trace does: "his moments" are clips that carry his player code, and "team moments while he played" are team goals and chances that overlap his tagged windows. Spiideo's story templates become one-click reel presets in the existing highlight studio: his match reel, team goals, first-half reel, and goals and saves only. A follow-the-player overlay can be built from manual keyframes on top of the existing drawing tool: the user taps him every few seconds and the app interpolates a circle or name label. That needs no detection model. Jersey reading is the only automatic method vendors use, and it misreads numbers even for them ([KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51401562)). Consumer recaps add the rest of the pattern. Google Photos Recap lets users **hide people or moments and regenerate** ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/12/03/google-photos-2025-recap/)). Strava's Year in Sport is private unless the user shares it and is built as chapters ([Strava support](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401959-your-year-in-sport)). Both justify a season-end story with hide-and-regenerate controls. A printed season book is a gap: no vendor source offers one.

## Goal timestamps alone unlock the most robust new analytics

Game state is the strongest "analytics" layer the archive can add, because it is exact wherever every goal has a timestamp. Opta reports each team's share of match time spent ahead, level and behind ([The Analyst](https://theanalyst.com/articles/premier-league-game-state-stats-winning-losing-2025-26)). StatsBomb treats the drawing state as closest to a team's natural style ([StatsBomb](https://statsbomb.com/articles/soccer/using-xpass-to-measure-the-impact-of-gamestate-on-team-style)). The app can rebuild the running score from the goal events in order: Veo goals carry period and video time, and Veo kick-off events give the offsets. From that it can derive a coloured ahead/level/behind strip under the video and the score state at every moment as a filter. It also yields comebacks and leads lost, half-time to full-time transitions, longest runs of unanswered goals, late equalisers and winners, and clean-sheet streaks. One rule makes this safe: if the goal events do not add up to the final score, mark the match "incomplete" and leave it out. Age groups play different half lengths, so compare seasons by share of match, not raw minutes.

Some outputs are honest only as counts. A first-goal record should read "scored first in 14, won 11", never "scoring first wins games". FiveThirtyEight shows scoring first largely reflects being the better team ([FiveThirtyEight](https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/does-scoring-first-in-the-nfl-make-you-a-good-team-or-do-good-teams-just-score-first)). Professional goals cluster late in each half ([arXiv 2501.18606](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.18606v1)). A youth season produces only single-digit counts per time bin, so a "when our goals came" chart is a memory, not a fitness claim. The same paper finds "bursty" scoring may only mean the stronger team keeps scoring, so a burst should read as a story ("four goals in ten minutes"), not as momentum.

Veo's team-sided restart events support rough territory signals that sit beside the existing momentum chart. These include shot share, corner share, an "attacking pressure (rough)" index built from own corners and opponent goal kicks, goals soon after a corner, an on-target proxy (a shot followed by a save, or by a goal kick), and estimated ball-in-play time from the gaps between out-of-play and restart events. None of these proxies has been validated. Pro field tilt is defined from final-third touches the app cannot see ([Driblab](https://www.driblab.com/blog/field-tilt-percentage-of-passes-and-touches-in-the-final-third)), so these must be labelled as rough. Two things should stay out of the UI. The first is shot conversion: team conversion barely repeats from season to season even in the Premier League (R^2 = 0.002) ([Fantasy Football Scout](https://www.fantasyfootballscout.co.uk/2017/07/10/the-plexiglas-principle-the-teams-set-to-improve-in-201718?hc_page=-1)). The second is plus-minus, which is noisy even with full professional lineups ([American Soccer Analysis](https://americansocceranalysis.com/home/2019/8/14/with-or-without-you-wowy-a-plus-minus-alternative-for-soccer)). With tag windows standing in for minutes, it would mostly measure tagging habits.

Ratings from scores (Elo, Glicko, Dixon-Coles) work best hidden. Most opponents appear one to three times a season, and one implementation flags any team with fewer than 4 matches as "barely estimated" ([MetricGate](https://metricgate.com/calculator/dixon-coles-soccer-model)). Glicko's rating deviation shows that uncertainty honestly ([fooskill docs](https://fooskill.readthedocs.io/en/latest/overview/rating_system.html)). The useful outputs are internal: an own-team form line with an uncertainty band that resets each season, a "best win" ranked by surprise, and "the team that gave us the hardest games", shown with the scores rather than a number. The FA bans publishing results and tables for U7-U11 ([The FA](https://www.thefa.com/news/2015/dec/09/fa-reiterates-youth-league-results-position-091215)). That supports keeping all of this private by default and never ranking the child against teammates.

## Narrative and search features compound the same facts

A templated match recap is Hudl's AI Insights idea without the cloud. For example: "Won 3-2 away at X, came from 2-0 down, half-time 1-2, he featured in 6 moments including a second-half save." Each clause appears only when its data exists. A local LLM, if one is added, should only rephrase a structured fact sheet, and the app should reject any output whose numbers are not in that sheet. The same facts feed several other features:

- **Season Wrapped story**: chapters such as first and last goals, biggest comeback, longest unbeaten run, the top moment and "goals by part of match". Each number carries a provenance line such as "from 18 tagged matches". This addresses the rule against presenting tag counts as definitive.
- **On this day**: needs only dates.
- **Development strip across age groups**: labels each format change (pitch size, players per side) so the strip is not read as like-for-like.
- **Smart playlists**: saved queries over match facets (season, opponent, result) and moment facets (code, half, score state, child involved). Examples: "equalisers and winners", "saves when level", "every goal featuring him", "first goal at each age group". They grow as auto-check brings in new matches. Natural-language search can translate a question into the same filter chips, so results stay exact.

Data-quality work makes all of this trustworthy:

- **Hudl/Veo duplicate detection**: same date plus a fuzzy opponent match, confirmed by score and duration. Merge the records, never delete files.
- **Score-conflict checks**: compare the Hudl, Veo and manually entered scores, check that half scores add up, and check that goal events match the final score.
- **Missing-tag detection**: flag matches with goals but no GOAL tags, and matches he played with no player tags.
- **Opponent alias table**: keyed by club, team variant and age group, because a club's U10 Reds and U10 Blues are different teams. Dixon-Coles-style models need consistent team identities to work at all ([regista](https://torvaney.github.io/regista)).

## Preservation and sharing deserve priority because Veo deletes the source

Veo deletes recordings automatically after **12 months on Team/Club/Enterprise plans and 6 months on Starter**, and offers no extension ([Veo support](https://support.veo.com/hc/en-us/articles/23485386875921)). The Veo parser already reads an availability status (`expires-soon`). The obvious step is an at-risk queue: each recording gets an expected expiry date, the download queue is sorted soonest-first, badges appear at 60, 30 and 7 days, and an `expired_at_source` record makes any loss visible. A recording should count as safe only once it is downloaded, checksummed and held in at least two locations.

The app already computes SHA-256 at import and has a manual verify job (spec 070). The Library of Congress asks for at least two copies far apart, a written description of the archive, yearly checks and fresh media every five years ([LoC](https://digitalpreservation.gov/personalarchiving/video.html)). NDSA Levels 2.0 gives a self-assessment ladder for these practices ([NDSA](https://ndsa.org/2019/10/28/ndsa-announces-the-levels-of-digital-preservation-2.0.html)). Building on what exists means four things:

- a rolling nightly re-verify of a few GB, across the Mac, the NAS and any other copies
- a per-file "copies and last verified" view
- per-season BagIt-compatible `manifest-sha256.txt` files ([RFC 8493](https://rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8493)) beside sidecar `match.json` files
- a root README, so the archive survives without the app

Keep the original MP4s as masters. FFV1/MKV is the LoC's preferred preservation format ([LoC](https://blogs.loc.gov/thesignal/2023/12/embracing-ffv1-matroska-container-preferred/)), but transcoding already-lossy H.264 to it adds size and recovers nothing. Event chapters (ffmpeg FFMETADATA, stream copy) ([ImageKit](https://imagekit.io/blog/add-chapters-to-mp4-videos/)) belong in the Plex derivative, never the master. Keep them to goals, saves and shots, because Jellyfin users report that very many chapters slow players ([Jellyfin forum](https://forum.jellyfin.org/printthread.php?tid=8848)). For an offsite copy, the owner rule forbids cloud upload of footage. A client-side-encrypted restic copy to Backblaze B2 (about USD 7/TB-month) arguably still breaks that rule ([Upstash](https://upstash.com/blog/backblaze-b2-pricing-explained-2026)). A rotated USB disk kept at a relative's house meets the LoC geographic-separation advice with no cloud at all. That choice goes to the owner as a decision, not something to build by default. Trickplay thumbnail strips would also make scrubbing a 90-minute match much faster in the web player.

Sharing needs guardrails before features. FA guidance names identification, meaning name plus image plus club, and re-sharing of privately shared footage as key risks ([FA 8.3](https://www.thefa.com/-/media/thefacom-new/files/rules-and-regulations/safeguarding/21-10-2025/83-photography-and-filming-children.ashx)). The ICO treats sharing with friends and family as household use, but a public audience brings data-protection duties, especially for other children ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-the-public/schools/photos/)). Since late November 2025, Plex charges for remote streaming of personal media ([Privacy Guides](https://www.privacyguides.org/news/2025/11/26/plex-begins-enforcing-new-restrictions-on-remote-streaming-this-week/)). That makes a self-hosted share path worthwhile. Each link covers one clip or playlist, never a full match by default, because full matches show many other children. Links use high-entropy tokens, expire after 30 days by default and can be revoked. They are reached over Tailscale rather than port forwarding, and any password goes in a POST body, never the URL. Immich's CVE-2026-25118 was exactly a shared-album password sent in URL query parameters ([NVD](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/cve-2026-25118)). Exports strip surname, club and venue from titles and container metadata. A restricted-players list blocks named teammates from shared output. A self-contained static HTML season index on a USB stick is the lowest-risk bundle for grandparents. A print-ready season yearbook can reuse the existing report PNGs, with QR codes pointing at relative paths on that stick rather than at hosted video.

## Vision models help only as native, suggest-only assistants

GPU work cannot run in Docker on macOS: Docker itself says there is "no GPU passthrough for Metal in containers" ([Docker](https://www.docker.com/blog/docker-model-runner-vllm-metal-macos/)). Any machine-learning job therefore belongs in the native helper as a queued worker. The cheapest signal is audio. PANNs, pretrained on 527 AudioSet classes ([arXiv 1912.10211](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10211v5)), runs on CPU and could score cheering and whistles each second to rank existing moments and surface untagged peaks. No one has validated it on touchline audio. Action spotting models such as T-DEED (GPL-3.0) are trained on broadcast footage ([GitHub](https://github.com/arturxe2/T-DEED)), and their accuracy drops clearly on amateur AI-camera footage ([CVPRW 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01304)). The app's own Veo/Hudl tags are better labels than these models produce. Tracking and vertical reframing are feasible with permissively licensed parts: RF-DETR Nano-Large is Apache 2.0, while Ultralytics YOLO is AGPL ([Roboflow](https://roboflow.com/model-licenses/rf-detr)). The pipeline is a manual tap on the child, a tracker, a smoothed crop window and ffmpeg. Veo panoramic files leave the most room to crop. Jersey OCR reaches 87.4% on professional tracklets ([arXiv 2405.13896](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13896v1)) and would do worse at grassroots, so it fits only as a re-acquire aid. It reads a printed number, which keeps it inside the no-biometrics rule. Whole-body re-ID embeddings drift toward biometric tracking and should be avoided. Whisper transcription (whisper.cpp, MIT, Metal) ([GitHub](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp)) works technically, but transcripts capture other families' voices and names. It ranks low.

## Conclusion

The research reverses the usual order of priority. Vendors build AI to produce the labels that Football Memories already imports. The scarce resources here are trust and survival: honest framing of noisy numbers, and making sure the footage still exists in ten years. The highest-value work is therefore the score-derived analytics, which are exact and need little data, and the expiry and fixity features, which protect a one-copy archive. Vision models come after those, as assistants whose output always passes through a human tap.

Three items need owner decisions before they are built: whether an encrypted offsite copy counts as "cloud upload", whether any public-audience sharing should exist at all, and whether a local LLM may rephrase recaps. Each should be recorded in `docs/owner-decisions/`. Several claims remain unverified: the accuracy of Veo's own event detection, whether Plex honours MP4 chapter atoms, and how well audio or jersey models perform on this specific footage. Each should be checked on a sample match before committing to a design.
